# Class Notes Style Guide

The master proofreading reference for *Peddie Chronicle* Class Notes. It collects the edits made most often, each with a wrong → right example.

- **Tone.** Class Notes are conversational, so edit more lightly than other Peddie publications.
- **Style.** Follow AP style, with some flexibility.

The **Checker** column says what the [Class Notes Checker](index.html) does with each rule:

- **Auto:** it flags the error and suggests a fix.
- **Flags:** it points to the spot, and you fix it.
- **By hand:** plain text can't show it, so you check it yourself.

## Mechanical rules

| Rule | Wrong | Right | Checker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **One space after periods.** Use a single space between sentences. | `…from you!␣␣Chris writes` | `…from you! Chris writes` | Auto |
| **Apostrophe before class years.** Use a true apostrophe (’), not an opening single quote (‘) or a straight quote ('). | Chris Sample ‘57 · Chris Sample '57 | Chris Sample ’57 | Auto |
| **No th/st/nd/rd on dates.** Use cardinal numbers. | May 15th · the 3rd of June | May 15 · June 3 | Auto |
| **Date and year commas.** Put a comma between the date and the year. If the sentence continues, put another comma after the year. | On May 15 1998 they married. · On May 15, 1998 they married. | On May 15, 1998, they married. | Auto |
| **Numbers.** Spell out one through nine. Use digits for 10 and above. Use commas in numbers of four digits or more. Spell out a number that starts a sentence, except a year. | 3 classmates · twelve grandchildren · 1000 miles · 20 classmates attended. | three classmates · 12 grandchildren · 1,000 miles · Twenty classmates attended. | Auto (sentence starts: Flags) |
| **Ages.** Always use digits, even under 10. Hyphenate an age used as a compound adjective. Set the age off with commas when it follows a name. | their two-year-old son · their 2 year old son · their son Tom 18 is… | their 2-year-old son · their son Tom, 18, is… | Auto (commas around ages: By hand) |
| **State abbreviations.** When a state follows a city, use the AP abbreviation, not the postal code. Put a comma after the state if the sentence continues. Never abbreviate Alaska, Hawaii, Idaho, Iowa, Maine, Ohio, Texas or Utah. Leave mailing addresses with ZIP codes as they are. | Newark, NJ with his family · Austin, TX · Austin, Tex. · Princeton, New Jersey | Newark, N.J., with his family · Austin, Texas · Princeton, N.J. | Auto |
| **City shortcuts.** NYC and LA may be abbreviated. | New York City | NYC | Optional suggestion (off by default) |
| **Word count.** Flag any submission over 2,000 words. | | | Auto: the checker warns per class-year section |
| **Degrees.** Abbreviated degrees take capitals and periods. Spelled-out degrees and Latin honors are lowercase. | PhD · BA · Master's degree · Magna Cum Laude | Ph.D. · B.A. · master’s degree · magna cum laude | Auto |
| **Reunion.** Lowercase it in general. Capitalize Alumni Weekend and a specific numbered Reunion. | See you at our Reunion · our 50th reunion · alumni weekend | See you at our reunion · our 50th Reunion · Alumni Weekend | Auto |

### AP state abbreviations (use with a city)

Ala., Ariz., Ark., Calif., Colo., Conn., Del., Fla., Ga., Ill., Ind., Kan., Ky., La., Md., Mass., Mich., Minn., Miss., Mo., Mont., Neb., Nev., N.H., N.J., N.M., N.Y., N.C., N.D., Okla., Ore., Pa., R.I., S.C., S.D., Tenn., Vt., Va., Wash., W.Va., Wis., Wyo.

**Never abbreviated:** Alaska, Hawaii, Idaho, Iowa, Maine, Ohio, Texas, Utah.

## Style rules

| Rule | Wrong | Right | Checker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bold alumni on first mention.** Bold each alum’s name the first time it appears in a class year section. For a member of the class being reported on, bold the name and leave off the class year. | Bill Daly ’55 (in the ’55 column) | **Bill Daly** | By hand |
| **No serial (Oxford) comma.** Leave out the comma before the conjunction in a simple series. Keep it only when the meaning would be unclear without it. | red, white, and blue | red, white and blue | Flags (check each one) |
| **Publication titles.** Put books, songs, movies, TV shows and albums in quotation marks, not italics. Newspapers and magazines take neither quotes nor italics. | *To Kill a Mockingbird* · 'The Long Way Home' · “The New York Times” | “To Kill a Mockingbird” · “The Long Way Home” · The New York Times | Partial: flags single-quoted titles and quoted newspaper names. Italics: By hand |
| **Multi-paragraph quotations.** When one speaker’s quote runs several paragraphs, start each paragraph with an opening quote mark. Put a closing mark only at the end of the last paragraph. | A paragraph that continues a quote but has no opening mark | “First paragraph… ⏎ “Second paragraph.” | Flags a quote that is never closed and isn't continued |
| **Double vs. single quotes.** Use double quotes for quoted material. Use single quotes only for a quote within a quote. | He sends ‘Aloha’ to all. | He sends “Aloha” to all. · She said, “He told me, ‘Come home.’” | Auto |
| **Family and friends.** Leave out the commas if the person has more than one such relative or friend. Use commas if there is only one. | his wife Nancy has… | his wife, Nancy, has… · Bob’s daughter Sarah (he has several daughters) | Flags spouses, partners, parents, and “eldest/youngest/only” relatives. Siblings and children: By hand, since only the writer knows how many there are |
| **Job titles.** Lowercase a title unless the complete formal title comes directly before the name. | Jane Smith, President of the company | President Jane Smith · Jane Smith, president of the company | Auto (common titles) |
| **Academic majors.** Lowercase majors unless the major contains a proper noun. | a History major · a Ph.D. in History · an english major | a history major · a Ph.D. in history · an English major | Auto |
| **Directions vs. regions.** Lowercase compass directions. Capitalize regions. | traveled West · the west coast | traveled west · the West Coast | Auto (common regions) |
| **Place names.** Include the state, province or country. Major cities stand alone: San Francisco, Dallas, Toronto, Paris. | They live in Lexington. | They live in Lexington, Ky. | By hand |

## Time sensitivity

Class Notes are due about two months before the issue comes out. An event described as upcoming may be over by publication.

- Flag future-tense events (“will attend,” “is planning to,” “upcoming,” “next month”) for the editor. **Checker:** Flags (no automatic fix).
- Adjust the tense to fit the publication date: “will attend” → “attended.”

## Also caught

The guide doesn't list these, but they come up in Class Notes and the checker flags them:

- Repeated words (the the), a space before punctuation, and doubled punctuation.
- A period in the middle of a sentence where a comma belongs: his wife, Carol. are doing well → his wife, Carol, are doing well.
- e-mail → email (AP).

## Still the editor’s call

The checker suggests; the editor decides. These stay with a person:

- Tone, voice and how lightly to edit.
- The spelling of names, and unclear names.
- Sensitive news: deaths, illness, divorce, and anything a family may not want printed.
- Whether a flagged serial comma is needed for clarity.
- Whether an upcoming event will have happened by publication.
